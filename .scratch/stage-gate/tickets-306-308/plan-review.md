## The B3 guard condition, reasoned through

The condition itself is **correct** on all four cases — unique worn (skip), absent
from DB (skip, conservative), same slot (no skip, identity swap runs), non-unique
worn (no skip, second placement simmed). Not inverted, no off-by-one.

The danger sits one level above it: per F1, not skipping does not *create* a row.
It enters the `best` competition and overwrites the identity-swap row — a silent
wrong answer of exactly the kind the project brief names as its worst case.

## Verdict

Two measurement failures re-run and refuted (F2, F3), and four structural findings
sharing one root: the plan treats the `wornAt` guard as the sole cause of 308's
limitation, and it is not. What the plan gets right and should carry forward
unchanged: the sequencing correction against the brief (F12), the five-step cycle
ordering (F14), and A4's handling of the touches-nothing-ported rule (F13).
